## Why these exist

Learner feedback: v0.5 added lick sounds artificial; v0.6 traditional chromatic descent is closer but still not convincingly musical. The problem appears structural: neither ending answered the song's opening motif convincingly. Analysis of classic acoustic blues guitar emphasized a motivated phrase, rhythmic space, independent thumb and melody, and a natural dominant-to-tonic cadence rather than a pasted scale run. See [reference analysis](../../../docs/music/reference-analysis-acoustic-blues-endings.md).

## v0.7A — Pedal note / descending inner voice

- Bar 11 (E7): open 1st string E as pedal over G-string fret 4 → 3 → 2 → 1 with **unequal rhythmic spacing**; final E7 upper chord anticipation. Fingerstyle thumb E–B anchors the measure.
- Bar 12 (B7 → E7): dominant chord, brief chordal reply; soft D upper pickup, half-beat breathing room, final E7 on offbeat after the bass resolves.
- **Hypothesis:** more harmonically colored, rooted in acoustic delta-blues two-voice turnarounds.
- **Risk:** descending chromatic line may feel too much like v0.6, even though rhythm and preparation are altered.

## v0.7B — Theme reprise / conversational bass

- Bar 11 (E7): open E7 chord, then the song's recognizable G→G♯ upper motive; the bass answers with a descending line E–D–C♯–C–B, leading directly into B7.
- Bar 12 (B7 → E7): short upper melodic answer, half-beat gap, final held E7 after bass anticipates the resolution.
- **Hypothesis:** stronger reference to the existing song and a more conversation-like ending, not a detached guitar lick.
- **Risk:** bass walk 5th string frets 4→3→2 needs to be physically tested for ease at 84 BPM.
